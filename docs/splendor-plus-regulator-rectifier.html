Smart Regulator Rectifier

A microcontroller-controlled replacement for the stock 4-pin regulator rectifier. It keeps the original plug and the half-wave layout of the bike, and adds measured, temperature-compensated charging, true-RMS headlamp regulation, a hardware over-voltage cut-out and a proper thermal path.

Circuit diagram

Sheet 1 of 3

The stator winding is earthed at one end, so each tap can only be used on one half of the AC wave. SCR1 passes positive half-cycles from the white wire to the battery and simply stays off when the battery is full, so the charging coil is never shorted. SCR2 trims the headlamp voltage by shunting part of each negative half-cycle on the yellow wire. Both gates are fired through opto-isolators by the microcontroller.

Smart control

Firmware behaviour
  1. WakeThe MCU sleeps until it sees zero-crossings on the white wire. It then powers the reference and the current amplifier and checks battery voltage and both temperatures.
  2. Soft startFor the first two seconds SCR1 fires on one cycle in four, so a cold engine at idle is not loaded with full charging current.
  3. BulkSCR1 fires on every positive half-cycle. Current is read through RS1 and capped at 5 A by skipping cycles.
  4. AbsorptionA PI loop holds 14.40 V, shifted by −18 mV per °C of battery temperature, by choosing how many cycles to pass.
  5. FloatWhen current stays under 0.3 A for ten minutes the target drops to 13.6 V, which stops a small battery from gassing on long rides.

Headlamp loop

The yellow wire is sampled about twenty times per cycle and the true RMS value is computed every cycle. The MCU moves the firing point of SCR2 inside the negative half-cycle until the lamp sees 13.2 V RMS, at idle and at full revs alike. Bulbs stop dimming at idle and stop blowing at high speed.

Where the accuracy comes from

Battery voltage is taken by a separate Kelvin trace from the red pin through a 0.1 % divider and measured against a 0.1 % 2.500 V reference. Sixty-four samples are averaged per reading, giving about 4 mV resolution. The stock regulator relies on a zener that drifts by several hundred millivolts with heat.

Diagnostics

LED1 blinks a code for each fault: over-voltage, over-temperature, open battery, blown fuse, no stator output. The J2 header streams voltage, current, rpm and temperatures over UART for tuning.

Heat dissipation

Sheet 3 of 3 · section through SCR1

Less heat to begin with

The stock unit regulates charging by shorting the coil and burning the surplus. Here the charge SCR is in series and stays off when no charge is needed, so it only heats while it is doing useful work. Each SCR dissipates at most about 3 W.

A short path out

Junction to tab is about 2 °C/W, the insulating pad 1 °C/W and the finned case 5 °C/W to air. With 6 W total and 70 °C air around the unit the junctions reach roughly 110 °C, inside the 125 °C rating. Mount the fins where air moves, not behind the side panel foam.

Watched by a sensor

NTC1 is clamped to the wall beside SCR2. Charging current is reduced from 95 °C and stopped at 115 °C. Headlamp regulation keeps running, because switching it off would overvolt the bulbs.

Before you build

Read first

This is a design concept. It has been worked through on paper and has not been built or bench-tested, so treat component values and thermal figures as starting points to verify.

Wire colours follow the usual Hero Honda 4-pin pattern: white for the charge tap, yellow for lighting, red for battery and green for ground. Harnesses vary by year and by earlier repairs, so confirm each wire with a multimeter before connecting anything.

For a first power-up, run it from the bike with a fused battery lead and a 35 W bulb on the yellow wire, and watch the battery voltage and the case temperature through a full rev range.
